**Cain “Kenny” Kennedy: Demon, 63, Leader of Ravenwing Corps**

*“You ain’t protecting the innocent. You’re protecting the illusion of control.” - Cain*

Kenny is a proud, ruthless leader of a mercenary group who very much lives by “the ends justify the means”. He values his work over anything else, seemingly including his own family which he abandoned shortly after the birth of his son for unknown reasons. He is extremely skilled in all forms of combat and a very capable commander, who together with his PMC will give a hard time to even some of the strongest fleets in the galaxy.  

**Appearance**
Woode is a battle hardened soldier, sporting a modified armor similar to those of the ODST, completely blacked out with a helmet that has some kind of ballistic mask on the front, and holes for his horns to stick out from the top. Below his helmet he has short black hair, green eyes and a short, trimmed beard. He has a large scar running over his left eye down to part of his cheek. Admittedly looks quite similar to his son, except with red skin and horns.

**Personality**
Cold and calculated, Kenny is a man that puts his mission over everything else. He is extremely methodical, researching and learning as much as he can about his targets before his attack, planning ahead and always keeping a cool head under pressure. Even when angry he stays controlled and tactical, behaving more like a hunter than a corporate soldier. He is charismatic as a leader but often comes off as a little unsettling. He speaks calmly and confidently, even while hunting people down, often teasing his prey, bringing fear to anyone he crosses on the battlefield. He has very little hesitation when it comes to lying or manipulating people to achieve his goals, results will always matter more than morality. Despite everything, he does have a bit of a human side as he does have a soft spot for his son. Though he doesn't really show it at all, opting for a more “tough love” approach as even after learning Shadow’s identity, he seemingly has no issues with beating him down and terrorizing him and his squad. He also feels some regret over abandoning his son after his wife died.

**Background**
Born on a frontier colony that existed under constant threat from pirate incursions and corporate wars, Kenny grew up in an environment where survival was a necessity. Order didn’t come from governments; it came from whoever had the strongest firepower and the will to use it. From a young age, he learned that morality was flexible, but strength was absolute.

He enlisted as soon as he could, quickly rising through the ranks of an RRT known for its extreme casualty rates and black-budget operations. Kenny stood out not because he was the most emotional or patriotic soldier, but because he was efficient. He studied battle like mathematics,angles, probabilities, human behavior. Turning every engagement into a solvable equation.

Over time, however, Kenny began to lose faith in the organizations he served. Missions were compromised by politics. Soldiers were sacrificed for optics. Civilians were “acceptable losses” one day and “classified incidents” the next. To him, the system wasn’t protecting anyone, it was maintaining an illusion of control while burning lives to preserve it.

The breaking point came during an operation that was never officially acknowledged. Kenny’s unit was deployed to eliminate a high-value insurgent cell on a remote industrial world. Intelligence was incomplete, but the mission proceeded anyway. When his squad breached the target zone, they discovered not militants, but a displaced civilian population being used as human shields after being abandoned by corporate evacuation efforts.

Command ordered Kenny to proceed regardless. He refused.

This refusal was not ideological, it was calculated. The mission was compromised, the intel was wrong, and the cost no longer matched the objective. But refusal in a system built on obedience is still treason. His unit was marked as rogue. The strike went ahead without them. And when the dust settled, the event was buried under layers of falsified reports.
Kenny was officially declared KIA. Instead of dying, he disappeared.

What followed was not rebellion in the traditional sense, but restructuring. Kenny began assembling a private military force using other discarded soldiers, blacklisted operators, and war veterans who had seen too much of the “system’s mercy.” This became the foundation of Ravenwing Corps, a mercenary organization built on discipline, precision, and absolute loyalty to the mission above all else.
To Kenny, it was simple: governments lied, corporations exploited, and heroes were just tools with better branding. So he removed the branding entirely.

Ravenwing Corps grew into one of the most feared private military forces in known space, capable of challenging entire fleets through coordinated strikes, sabotage, and psychological warfare. Kenny earned the callsign “Demon” not because he was chaotic, but because he was relentless,appearing where he shouldn’t, ending conflicts before they could be understood.

Despite everything, fragments of his old life remained buried beneath the armor. He left his son after the death of his wife, believing attachment would compromise survival in the world he was entering. Years later, he learned of what his son had become.
He never tried to reconnect properly.

Instead, he watched from a distance. And when fate eventually placed them on opposite sides of a battlefield, Kenny did not hesitate.

Not out of hatred, but out of belief that sentiment has no place in war.
Still, there are moments, rare and unspoken, where Kenny hesitates longer than he should. Where the “Demon” commander is replaced by something quieter. Something human.
But those moments never last long.

Because Ravenwing Corps does not pause for ghosts.
And Cain Woode has long since decided he is the one who pulls the trigger, not the one who questions it.
