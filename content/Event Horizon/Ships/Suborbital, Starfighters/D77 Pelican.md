# Overview  
The **D77 Pelican** is a rugged, atmosphere-capable dropship developed by **Exteria Corporation** as a low-cost, mass-producible troop transport. It is slower and far less discreet than the **Prowler-class Dropship**, but it is cheaper to build and easier to maintain. It also carries ground vehicles, which the Prowler cannot do well, and its simple frame is tolerant of the abuse that line units give it.

Two variants are in service. The **D77-TC** is the standard troop carrier with an enclosed passenger bay and rear ramp. The **D77H-TCI** trades seating for heavy-lift clamps, letting it sling a vehicle under its fuselage for insertion into hostile zones. Both launch from carriers and frigates, since the Pelican has no slipspace drive of its own.

# History  
The Pelican was authorized after Fleet Command criticized the Prowler as too expensive to serve as a standard troop transport. The Prowler's stealth systems, baffled drives, and slipspace capability suit it to infiltration work, but they make it impractical to field in large numbers for ordinary assaults. Exteria's answer was a simpler craft intended to carry a full squad and its equipment into a landing zone, with survivability and numbers taking priority over stealth.

Early prototypes were tested in secret, and the type was first shown to the wider fleet at the start of the **Demonic War** with the Obsidian Dominion. Its first sustained combat use came during the **Reclamation Campaigns**. Pelicans launched from the **UCG Nautilus** and her sister ships of the **Stalwart-class Sub-Fleet** delivered Marines and armor during the **Battle of Vorath Prime**, and the crews reports were generally positive.

Production has stayed limited and uneven, so most Pelicans are still assigned to a few frontline carriers and frigates rather than widely distributed. During the **Fall of Nardia**, UCG forces pulled the Pelicans from an **Epoch-class** carrier and refitted the hangars for **Sabre** launch systems. Pilots remember it as a sign of how scarce the type still was.

# Military Significance  
The Pelican fills a gap that the Prowler was never meant to fill. A Prowler carries 20 passengers but needs a five-person specialist crew and costs far more to operate. A Pelican carries 16 troops with a crew of four, and its internal bay can instead hold an **M12 Force Application Vehicle**. The **D77H-TCI** can sling a vehicle under the hull for a drop that would otherwise need an Albatross freighter or a drop-pod barrage.

The **M370 chin autocannon** and **M247H door guns** provide suppressive fire while troops disembark, and the optional **M41 rocket pods** give it limited strike capability. It is not a gunship, and crews are trained to leave the zone quickly once the ramp is clear.

Current planning has Pelicans eventually replacing Prowlers as the standard troop transport. The Prowler is expected to stay in service for **ODST**, **Epsilon**, and other elite or covert units, where stealth and independent slipspace travel remain worth the expense.

# Cultural Significance  
Marines and ODSTs call the Pelican the **"Big Bird,"** and the nickname has grown more affectionate as more crews have survived hot drops in them. Compared with the Prowler's "Ghost Taxi" reputation, the Pelican is seen as the dependable workhorse. It isn't glamorous, but it reliably gets troops and vehicles in and out of a fight.

Pilots who fly the type early in its service are seen as pioneers, and a few units have painted their Pelicans with nose art in the tradition of old-world bomber crews.

# Current Status  
The Pelican is **experimental but expanding**. It is still uncommon, and most UCG personnel will go through a whole tour without riding in one. Even so, more are appearing in carrier and frigate complements each quarter, and the **Stalwart-class** and **Epoch-class** hangars list them as standard equipment. Exteria is working on improved armor, a more efficient engine layout, and an extended-range variant, and Fleet Command expects the Pelican to become the main transport for conventional forces.

Other factions have shown interest in the design, though the Pelican's capacity and cost make it attractive mostly to forces that need to move many troops cheaply rather than quietly.
