### Repulsion Shield
**Class:** Adaptive Electromagnetic Induction Defense System  
**User:** Sydney "Hazard" Navarro  
**Platform:** Modified ODST Battle Armor  
**Base Specs:** Proximity defense, directional force control, short-duration area denial

**Standard Configuration:**
- Conductive armor mesh integrated through the torso, limbs, and helmet
- Distributed point-charge emitters embedded beneath the armor plating
- Independent reserve battery for emergency operation
- HALO upgraded with a secondary processor dedicated to field management

**Operating Principle:**
- The armor charges selected portions of the surrounding space with controlled point charges. Objects entering the field experience stronger electromagnetic repulsion as they approach Sydney.
- The shield acts as a dampening field rather than an invisible wall. Incoming matter is slowed and pushed away, behaving more like a bullet passing through syrup than striking a rigid barrier.
- The field is directional and localized, concentrating force where HALO identifies an immediate collision, impact, or hostile approach instead of maintaining a full sphere.

**HALO Integration:**
- HALO handles threat detection, charge distribution, and rapid field shaping while the primary armor systems remain available to Sydney.
- It activates only the emitters covering areas of danger, conserving energy and preventing the shield from resisting harmless movement or allies. Sydney can override this behavior, but manual expansion increases power draw.

**Applications:**
- Deflecting or slowing bullets, debris, blades, and close-range attackers
- Pushing nearby combatants or obstacles away to create an opening
- Providing controlled lift or extra reach against the ground or a nearby wall
- Shaping a weak inverse field to pull an ally toward Sydney for emergency extraction

**Limitations:**
- The shield is not absolute protection. Large masses, high-energy attacks, and objects already inside the field can exceed its available force or leave too little reaction time.
- Full-area operation rapidly drains the reserve battery and places thermal stress on the conductive mesh.
- Multiple simultaneous vectors, complex electromagnetic environments, or attacks from within the field can force HALO to divide its output.
- The inverse field is for retrieving people or equipment, not for safely yanking an unwilling target at maximum output.

**Notes:**
- The system is an armor modification rather than a conventional shield generator. Sydney's gauntlets use the same charge architecture at much higher local intensity, allowing both systems to function as one force-control platform.
